# ADR-0005: Span representation conventions

Date: 2026-10-24 · Status: accepted (director, 2026-10-24; verified consistent with ARCHITECTURE.md)

## Decision
1. `ArcSpan` stores centre, radius, start angle, end angle and an `ArcDirection` (CCW/CW). The swept angle is always taken in (0, 2π]: coincident start/end angles (mod 2π) denote a **full circle**. A zero-length arc therefore cannot be represented; degenerate pieces are `LineSpan`s.
2. `Span` is `std::variant<LineSpan, ArcSpan, BezierSpan>` with free functions (`length`, `pointAt`, `bounds`, `reversed`, `transformed`, `polygoniseInto`) rather than a class hierarchy.
3. `transformed(Span, Transform2)` returns a `std::vector<Span>`. Lines and Beziers always map to one span. An arc stays an arc when the transform is conformal (rotation, uniform scale, mirror, translation; mirroring flips its direction). Under any other affine map (non-uniform scale, shear) the arc is first converted to cubic Beziers of ≤ 90° each, which are then transformed exactly.
4. Polygonisation appends the end point of each span but not its start, so spans chain without duplicates; a closed `Contour` drops the repeated closing vertex and relies on `Polyline::closed`.
5. Span parameters `t ∈ [0,1]` are arc-length-linear for lines and arcs but the native curve parameter for Beziers. `Contour::pointAt` and `Polyline::pointAt` are arc-length-linear over the whole chain.

## Rationale
- Representing a full circle as a single span keeps circle objects, circle snapping and "Only Circles" filters simple; the (0, 2π] rule removes the ambiguity between "no sweep" and "full sweep" without an extra flag.
- A variant keeps spans as plain copyable values with no virtual dispatch, which suits Clipper2 interop, undo snapshots and serialisation.
- Stretching a circle non-uniformly produces an ellipse, which no span type represents exactly; converting to Beziers keeps the operation closed over the span set and bounded in error (standard 4/3·tan(θ/4) construction, radial error ≈ 2.7e-4·r per 90°). Returning a list makes the span-count change explicit at the call site.

## Consequences
- Downstream code must not assume one-span-in/one-span-out for transforms; `Contour::transform` already handles the expansion.
- Arc fitting after offsets/booleans (T-0003, Fit Curves) should produce `ArcSpan`s with the same angle convention.
- Zero-length spans are detected with `isDegenerate(span, tol)` and cleaned by later cards (Vector Validator).

## Appendix A2 — Work bounds of the Bezier routines (T-0178)

Every recursive or subdividing Bezier routine has an explicit bound and a stated behaviour when it is hit.

| Routine | Bound | When it is spent |
|---|---|---|
| `BezierSpan::length(relTol)` | Pass 1 (the original Gravesen subdivision, depth ≤ 40) examines ≤ 2^19 pieces; pass 2 ≤ 2^20 pieces. | Pass 1 gives up and pass 2 runs; pieces left in pass 2 take their unrefined estimate (best effort). |
| `BezierSpan::length`, flatness | A piece with `polygon − chord ≤ 8·ε·(largest coordinate of the piece)` stops (pass 2 only): below that the flatness is rounding noise. Pass 2 measures the curve relative to its first control point, so the noise is that of its size, not its position. | — |
| `BezierSpan::length`, range | Control values beyond 1e100 are scaled by an exact power of two and the result scaled back; the result is `+inf` when it exceeds the double range. | — |
| `bounds()` | Closed form (no recursion). Control values beyond 1e100 are scaled before the discriminant is formed; if evaluating the extrema still overflows, the control-polygon box (which contains the curve) is returned. The result contains the curve down to extents of about 1e-11 mm; sub-picometre curves may collapse to their end points, as before, because the derivative quadratic's coefficients are compared with an absolute 1e-14 (irrelevant at CNC sizes). | — |
| `closestPoint(BezierSpan, p)` | Quintic root isolation, depth ≤ 60. Curve and point beyond 1e100 are scaled by a power of two (`t` unchanged). | — |
| `polygoniseInto` / `polygoniseRegionInto` (Bezier) | Depth ≤ 24 and ≤ 2^20 splits per curve. | The remaining pieces become single chords: the polygon is coarser than `tol.chordal`, still ends at the curve's end. |
| `polygoniseInto` / `polygoniseRegionInto` / `ArcSpan::segmentCountFor` (arc, T-0182) | ≤ `kMaxArcSegments` = 2^20 equal chords per arc. A non-finite arc (NaN/infinite centre, radius or angles) gets 1 chord. | The count is cut to the cap: the polygon is coarser than `tol.chordal` (deviation of a chord ≈ radius·(sweep/2^20)²/8, e.g. 4.5 mm for a half circle of radius 1e12) and still ends at the arc's end point. |
| `intersect(Span, Span)` sampling / piece split | Depth ≤ 16 (≤ 65 537 samples) / depth ≤ 10 (≤ 1024 pieces) per Bezier. | Pieces at the cap are used as they are. |
| `intersect(Span, Span)`, Bezier pairs (T-0180) | An explicit work budget, `IntersectLimits::pairWork` (default 5·10^8 units, about 1-2 s of Release CPU; see below). | The result has `status == Truncated` and holds the hits found before the budget ran out. |
| `intersect(Contour, Contour)`, `selfIntersections`, `hasSelfIntersections` (T-0180) | `IntersectLimits::totalWork` (default 2·10^9 units) over all span pairs of the call, each pair also under `pairWork`. | `status == Truncated`; `hasSelfIntersections` answers true. |
| Non-finite input (T-0180) | Any span with a NaN or infinite coordinate or arc parameter is refused before anything is computed. | `status == InvalidInput`, empty result (`hasSelfIntersections` true). |
| Magnitude (T-0180) | Any span with a coordinate or arc radius beyond `kMaxIntersectMagnitude` = 1e12 mm is refused before anything is computed. | `status == TooLarge`, empty result (`hasSelfIntersections` true). Priority InvalidInput over TooLarge over Truncated. |

Arc polygonisation (T-0182): before the cap the chord count was proportional to radius / chordal tolerance without limit (a radius of 1e12 at the default tolerance made 70 million points in 2.6 s; larger radii ran out of memory, and when `1 - tol/radius` rounds to 1 the count was an undefined float-to-integer conversion). Now `segmentCountFor` returns at most 2^20 and `segmentCountIsCapped(tol)` tells whether it cut the count (also true for a non-finite arc). `polygoniseInto`, `polygoniseRegionInto` and `Contour::polygonise` take an optional `bool* coarse` that they set (never reset) when the polygon is coarser than the tolerance: an arc past the cap, a Bezier past its depth/split cap, or a non-finite span. Measured (Release, one full circle, tolerances 1e-3 and 1e-9): radii 1e9, 1e10, 1e11, 1e12, 1e15, 1e100, 1e300, 1.7e308 all give 1 048 576 points in 0.024-0.038 s CPU (inside `ulimit -v 400000`); an infinite radius gives 1 point in 0 s. Before: 2.2 M / 7.0 M / 22 M / 70 M points in 0.09 / 0.21 / 0.72 / 2.6 s for 1e9..1e12 at 1e-3. Arcs that need no more than 2^20 chords are bit-identical to before (hash of the points of 21 720 arcs: radii 1e-6..1e9, both directions, full circles, tiny sweeps, centres up to 5e8 from the origin, tolerances 1e-9..1, open and closed contour paths, identical on main and the branch).

Callers. Those where a coarse polygon only costs fidelity use the capped result: drawing (`buildCanvasScene`), selection hit tests and the marquee, snapping, `medialAxis` winding rings, the V-carve skeleton check, edit operations and span intersection (through `segmentCountFor`). Those whose output must meet the tolerance refuse: `toClipperPath`, used by `booleanOp`/`unionContours`, `offsetContours` and `OpenPathClipper`, leaves a contour with a coarse polygon out (as degenerate input is) and counts it: `BooleanResult::refusedContours`, `OpenPathClipper::refusedContours()`. Offsetting open contours by spans (`offsetOpenContour`) keeps arcs as arcs and only polygonises Beziers, which were already bounded. `medialAxis` (the one caller that indexes by coordinates) ignores a contour with a non-finite coordinate, radius or angle, or a coordinate or radius beyond 1e12 mm in magnitude (the importer's cap; exactly ±1e12 is accepted), promptly: it is counted in `ignoredContours`, and a finite contour ignored for its magnitude also sets `accuracyCapped`. Before, a radius of about 1e100 made it hang and 1.7e308 crashed (a NaN converted to an index); a region at an offset of 1e12 mm is now ignored where it used to be computed with `accuracyCapped` set.

Refused contours (T-0183): `refusedByClippers(contour, tol)` / `refusedContourIndices(contours, tol)` (boolean.hpp) answer, in O(spans) for lines and arcs (a contour with a Bezier is polygonised once), whether the clippers would refuse a contour at `tol`; they agree with `BooleanResult::refusedContours` (tested over arcs, windings, NaN arcs and Beziers at three tolerances). The toolpath operations use them to report a `ContourRefused` diagnostic per vector (ADR-0018 item 5) and the DXF and SVG importers use them (at the import tolerance) to warn "arc or curve too large to approximate within the tolerance"; the contour is kept in the drawing.

Accuracy of `length`: the result is within `relTol` (default 1e-10) of the true length plus the coordinate rounding noise (~ `ε · position`). Pass 1 is bit-identical to the pre-T-0178 code and is used when the largest coordinate is ≤ 1e4 × the control polygon's length and it finishes within its piece budget; every other curve (position/size above 1e4, where the old scheme could run for seconds or not terminate) takes pass 2, whose values differ from the old ones by the old scheme's own rounding noise (≤ ~1e-5 relative in the sampled corpus, typically 1e-12).

`intersect(Span, Span)` for two Beziers is polynomial in the sample and piece counts above; the near-coincident long-curve case (two loops of extent 1e6 at the default tolerance: ~1.6·10^4 raw hits) is no longer quadratic in the hits (hit merging uses a hash grid with the linear scan's first-match semantics, `hit_index.hpp`; the touch-settling trail and minima are ordered trees, not sorted vectors). Results are unchanged; CPU time for that pair fell from 5.2 s to about 0.5 s (0.47-0.56 s over several runs, Release).

### T-0180: the work budget of the Bezier intersection

T-0178 left `intersect(Bezier, Bezier)` bounded only by the per-curve caps, which is polynomial but large (pairs of near-coincident loops took 30-44 s at extent 1e7..1e8 and 125 s at 1e100, NaN/infinite spans hung, and 10 of the 12 000 random pairs of the verifier's corpus took over 20 s). T-0180 adds three things.

**1. A deterministic work budget.** The Bezier path of `intersect(Span, Span)` counts work in abstract units: a chord-pair box test is 1, a Bezier chord sample 24, a piece of a cut Bezier 64, building a box index 4 per box and tree level, each node a box-index query visits 1, a curve evaluation or nearest-point step 64, a Newton refinement 256, a walk step through the minima tree 6 per node visited, a hit comparison in the merge 4, a sample of a huge arc 8 (charged before the samples are made). A unit is 2.4-5.4 ns of Release CPU depending on the mix (measured on a loaded 2020s machine: 2.4 ns for pair work dominated by nearest-point walks, 5.4 ns for contour self-intersection dominated by chord-index queries), so a pair costs up to about 3 s and a contour call (`totalWork`, four pairs' worth) at most about 5-11 s of CPU, measured at 5-11 s on contours of 30 Beziers with 1e9..1e12 control points whose unmetered index builds and chord samples had cost 76-150 s in round 2 (T-0180 verification: they were charged one unit each, at 40 ns real cost). Counting units instead of reading a clock makes a truncated result reproducible and independent of the machine. The loops that can run long check the meter and stop; what has been found is kept. When candidate generation spends the pair's budget, the settling and merging of the candidates found so far get a reserve of one eighth and one sixteenth of it, so a truncated result is the best the budget buys and not empty; the work after the generation phase is therefore at most 1.19 times `pairWork` (the reserves add 3/16 of it). `lastPairWork` can exceed even that: the samples of a huge arc are charged up front, in one step, so a pair that is truncated by that charge reports the whole charge. `IntersectLimits` is passed to the overloads that take it; the others use the defaults (`pairWork` 5·10^8, `totalWork` 2·10^9), which a test can replace for a scope (`detail::ScopedDefaultLimits`, `intersect_defaults.hpp`). Line and arc pairs are closed-form and not metered.

**2. Truncation is reported, never silent.** `IntersectionResult` and `ContourIntersections` carry `status` (`Complete`, `Truncated`, `InvalidInput`) and `complete()`. A truncated result contains only genuine hits (each verified against both spans) but may miss some. Callers: `trimToBoundary`, `trimSpansAt` and `extendSpan` (edit_ops) refuse with the new `EditStatus::TooComplex` and change nothing; `hasSelfIntersections` answers true when it cannot rule a self-intersection out, and V-carve treats a pair or a self test that is not complete as a possible crossing (the vectors are resolved together with the exact union, which is also right for vectors that do not cross); snapping uses the hits it gets (best effort, a suggestion the user sees). Offset, boolean and the medial axis do not call `intersect` (they work on polygons through Clipper2 and their own routines), so they need no handling.

**3. Non-finite and absurdly large spans are refused up front** (`InvalidInput` / `TooLarge`, empty result) in every entry point, for all span kinds, before `isZeroLength`/`length()` or any sampling can run on them. The magnitude cap, 1e12 mm on every coordinate and arc radius, is the cap `edit_ops` already uses (T-0208): CNC drawings are below 1e9 mm, and beyond ~1e12 the squares inside the quintic nearest-point search (`closestPoint`) and the chord sampling overflow or lose all precision. Round 1 of the verification measured the consequence: a finite Bezier with control points at 1.7e308 against itself took 7-29 s *within* the budget, because the global nearest-point search cost orders of magnitude more than its charge. The cap removes that region (it also makes the huge-arc sampling problem of `ArcSpan::segmentCountFor`, which casts an infinite or NaN count when chordal/R drops below an ulp (R above ~1e14), unreachable from `intersect`; that function is T-0182's), and the meter now charges the true cost of every global nearest-point search: `closestPoint(BezierSpan)` counts its own work (16 units per quintic subdivision step, 4 per bisection step, 128 per call; `closest_work.hpp`) and `intersect` charges the count, instead of a flat 512 per search. At the cap the costs are the ordinary ones (`spiky` curve at 1e12 against itself, loops and huge curves at 1e12 are in the tests and the table).

**What made most of the old cases fast was not the budget but two quadratic loops.** The chord-pair loops (A's chords against B's chords per piece pair, and the chords of one Bezier against each other for self-intersections) tested every pair, 65 537 x 65 537 = 2·10^9 pairs for the long curves, and the self-intersection loop paid a `hypot` per pair for pairs far apart. They now go through the box index of `box_index.hpp` when there are more than 4096 pairs (64 chords for the self loop): the pairs and their order are the same (the index only skips pairs whose boxes do not meet; the self loop grows each chord's box by half its own length so that however `segmentProximity` rounds its division, no pair it could report a crossing for is skipped). Results are bit-identical. Contour-level calls find their span pairs the same way and drop duplicate hits through `HitIndex` instead of an `any_of` scan; points outside the hash grid's range (extents beyond 1e15 cells) are scanned linearly and charged to the budget.

Measured, Release, CPU seconds on a machine at load average ~20 (about 1.5-2x inflated; `before` is main at 3586981, `after` this change with the defaults):

On the 12 000-pair corpus and a 10 000-pair tie-heavy corpus the result hashes (points, tangent flags, order, overlaps, contour hits, polygonisation) are identical to main on every pair that finished there, and the two corpus pairs that timed out on main now finish. Ten thousand spans of a contour pair (10 000 crossings) take 13 ms and the cost is linear to within the log factor (4.9x for 4x the spans), and 5 000 spans of a self-crossing contour 12 ms.

Remaining limits and caveats:
- Unit counts depend on floating-point decisions (Newton iteration counts, which branch a nearest point takes), so a 1-ulp difference in libm between compilers or platforms can flip `Complete` and `Truncated` for inputs that land within a few units of 5·10^8 (pair) or 2·10^9 (contour). The reference unit counts in `bezier_intersect_bounds_test.cpp` have a band of +-12%, and +-2% / +-1.5% for the two pairs dominated by Newton refinements and by nearest-point searches, which pins `kRefine` and the step costs of `closestPoint` (changing them by 15-25% leaves the other pairs inside their bands but not these).
- A pair's budget is `min(pairWork, what is left of totalWork)`, but the pair's reserve phases can run past it, and the last pair of a contour call can overshoot a nearly exhausted total by up to its own pair budget (about 1.19 `pairWork` plus any up-front arc-sample charge).
- Adversarial *line* pairs are slower than on main by 2.5-3x and quadratic either way: 5,000 lines crossing in one 1e-4 cell take 4.1 s to intersect (1.6 s on main); 10,000 long parallel diagonals take 8.6 s for `selfIntersections` (6.8 s on main), 64 s at 40,000 spans; an n x n crossing grid is output-quadratic (1.56 million hits in 8 s). Lines and arcs are closed-form and are not metered, so these are bounded by nothing but their size (the extra cost is the hit index and box query on every hit).
- The budget bounds CPU, not memory: one pair can hold up to a few million candidates (tens of megabytes). The touch-settling minima query (`GapMinima::lowestIn`) still scans every minimum inside a bracket, so near-coincident long loops of extent 1e7 and above truncate rather than complete; making it logarithmic is future work. A hit list that was cut short is a prefix of nothing in particular (the best candidates found first are kept, not the first along the curve). The T-0208 extent guard in `edit_ops` is not relaxed by this change: the intersections it protects are now bounded, but the other costs behind the guard (`BoundarySet` classification, the arc probe of `extendSpan`) are not covered by the budget (see T-0179).

The 2^20 piece cap of `BezierSpan::length` pass 2 is a defensive bound that no finite input reaches (the noise floor stops a size-1 loop after ~2^17 pieces at any scale), so it has no test.
